When you look at the stars, you’re looking back in time

Light has a finite speed. The light reaching your eyes from a distant object began its journey in the past.

The Moon is about a second away

Moonlight reflected toward Earth takes roughly 1.3 seconds to arrive. Looking at the Moon means seeing it as it was a little over a second earlier.

Sunlight takes minutes

Light from the Sun takes about eight minutes and twenty seconds to reach Earth, on average.

Stars are much farther

Astronomers use light-years to describe distance: one light-year is the distance light travels in one year. A star ten light-years away is seen as it was about ten years ago.

A window into the universe

Telescopes let astronomers study light that has travelled for years, centuries or longer, giving us information about cosmic history.
