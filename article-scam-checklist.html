How to spot a suspicious message before you click

Scam messages often try to rush you. A short pause and a few checks can help you decide whether a message deserves your trust.

TrendSpark Editorial · Practical guide · Updated October 2026

This guide is for everyday awareness. No single clue proves a message is fraudulent; look at the whole situation and verify through a separate, trusted channel.

1. Check what the message wants you to do

Be cautious when an unexpected message asks you to sign in, reveal a one-time password (OTP), share bank details, install an app, scan a QR code or pay a fee immediately. Legitimate organizations should not need you to disclose your password or OTP to a person in a chat.

2. Inspect the sender and the destination

Display names can be copied. Check the complete email address or phone number, and read a link's domain carefully before opening it. A familiar brand name in a long or misspelled domain does not make the site official. On a computer, hover over a link to preview its destination; on a phone, press and hold only if you can do so safely without opening it.

3. Treat urgency as a reason to slow down

Messages claiming your account will be closed in minutes, a parcel is stuck, or a relative needs secret emergency money are designed to bypass careful thinking. Contact the organization or person using a number or website you find independently—not contact details supplied in the suspicious message.

4. Don't trust attachments just because you expected a document

Unexpected files, app-install links and requests to enable macros can carry risk. Confirm with the sender through another channel before opening an unusual attachment. Keep your operating system, browser and security tools updated.

5. Verify payments separately

Before sending money or changing payment details, call a known number or use the official app. Never share an OTP to “receive” money. If someone asks you to scan a QR code or approve a payment, read the action shown on your screen before confirming.

If you already clicked

Close the page and don't enter more information. If you entered a password, change it from the genuine website and change it anywhere else you reused it. If you shared financial details or sent money, contact your bank or payment provider immediately. In India, you can report cyber financial fraud through the official helpline 1930 and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal; use independently verified official channels.

A 20-second checklist

  • Was I expecting this message?
  • Is the sender and domain exactly right?
  • Is it pressuring me to act or keep a secret?
  • Can I verify it using the official app or a known phone number?

If any answer worries you, stop and verify before acting.
